# Writing Review Checklist

Use this checklist to review writing for clarity, structure, logic, and authenticity. Check off items as you verify them. Special emphasis on detecting AI writing signs.

## General Writing Review

- [ ] **Possessives**: Singular nouns use 's (e.g., "Charles's friend"); no apostrophe for plurals like "its."
- [ ] **Series and lists**: Commas after each term except the last (e.g., "red, white, and blue").
- [ ] **Parenthetic expressions**: Enclosed in commas (e.g., "The best way, unless pressed for time, is to travel on foot").
- [ ] **Co-ordinate clauses**: Comma before conjunctions like "and" or "but" (e.g., "The situation is perilous, but there is still one chance").
- [ ] **Independent clauses**: Not joined by comma alone; use semicolon or period.
- [ ] **Participial phrases**: Refer to grammatical subject (e.g., avoid "Walking slowly, the woman was accompanied by two children" if mismatched).
- [ ] **Active voice**: Preferred over passive for directness (e.g., "I remembered" vs. "It was remembered by me").
- [ ] **Positive form**: Statements in positive; avoid evasions (e.g., "He came late" vs. "He was not very often on time").
- [ ] **Specific language**: Use concrete, definite words (e.g., "It rained every day for a week" vs. "A period of unfavorable weather set in").
- [ ] **Conciseness**: Omit needless words (e.g., "He is a man who" → "He"; "owing to the fact that" → "since").
- [ ] **Sentence variety**: Avoid succession of loose sentences; mix structures.
- [ ] **Parallel construction**: Similar ideas in similar form (e.g., "He came, saw, and conquered").
- [ ] **Word placement**: Related words together; emphatic words at end.
- [ ] **Tense consistency**: One tense in summaries.
- [ ] **Punctuation mastery**: Rules applied consistently without errors.

## Paragraph Review

- [ ] **Unity**: Each paragraph focuses on one idea or topic.
- [ ] **Coherence**: Logical bridges (idea carryover, parallel sentences) and verbal bridges (repeated keywords, pronouns, transitions).
- [ ] **Topic sentence**: Clear indication of paragraph's main idea, preferably near the beginning.
- [ ] **Adequate development**: Fully elaborated with examples, data, anecdotes, definitions, comparisons, analysis, or chronology; avoid short, underdeveloped paragraphs.
- [ ] **Transitions**: Smooth links between paragraphs using signposts or transitional sentences.
- [ ] **New paragraph starts**: For new ideas, contrasts, reader pauses, or introductions/conclusions.

## Logic and Structure Review

- [ ] **Clear premises**: Each premise laid out explicitly in arguments.
- [ ] **Evidence provided**: Concrete support for premises (e.g., data, quotes, examples).
- [ ] **Conclusion connection**: Direct link from premises to conclusion (e.g., avoid assuming agreement).
- [ ] **Logical flow**: Arguments build step-by-step; no jumps or assumptions.
- [ ] **Avoid fallacies**: No vague claims, overgeneralizations, or unsupported opinions.
- [ ] **Syllogism structure**: Premise 1 + Premise 2 → Conclusion, expanded with elaboration.

## AI Writing Detection

- [ ] **Content emphasis**: No undue focus on significance, legacy, or trends (e.g., avoid overhyping minor topics).
- [ ] **Notability checks**: Significance not based solely on media coverage or vague attributions.
- [ ] **Depth of analysis**: Avoid superficial treatments; ensure nuanced engagement.
- [ ] **Language tone**: No promotional or sales-like enthusiasm (e.g., avoid "revolutionary" for mundane topics).
- [ ] **Attributions**: Specific sources; avoid vague "many experts" or overgeneralized opinions.
- [ ] **Conclusions**: No outline-style summaries of challenges/prospects.
- [ ] **Buzzwords overuse**: Limit "revolutionize," "transformative," "innovative," "dynamic," "landscape," "ecosystem," "narrative," "discourse," "paradigm," "leverage."
- [ ] **Simple phrases**: Use "is/are" when appropriate; avoid complex rephrasing unnecessarily.
- [ ] **Parallelisms**: No excessive negative structures (e.g., "not only... but also").
- [ ] **Rule of three**: Lists not forced into threes without reason.
- [ ] **Elegant variation**: Avoid excessive synonym use; repetition for clarity is fine.
- [ ] **False ranges**: No artificial "from X to Y" spectra.
- [ ] **Capitalization**: Appropriate sentence case; no inappropriate title case.
- [ ] **Formatting**: Minimal boldface, em dashes, emoji in formal text.
- [ ] **Structure**: Information in prose, not forced tables/lists.
- [ ] **Quotation marks**: Straight or curly as per style; consistent.
- [ ] **Collaborative phrases**: No "let's explore" or reader-engagement templates.
- [ ] **Disclaimers**: No knowledge-cutoff notes or source-gap speculations.
- [ ] **Markup issues**: Correct wikitext; no broken links, invalid citations, or fake DOIs/ISBNs.
- [ ] **Style shifts**: Consistent human-like writing; no abrupt changes.
- [ ] **Citations**: Valid, relevant references; no outdated dates or search links.
- [ ] **List overuse**: Avoid overuse of bulleted or numbered lists; consider prose for short lists.
- [ ] **Em dash overuse**: Avoid overuse of em dashes (—)
- [ ] **Emojis in formal writing**: Do not use emojis in formal writing
- [ ] **Overall authenticity**: Text predates AI tools if possible; editor can explain choices.
