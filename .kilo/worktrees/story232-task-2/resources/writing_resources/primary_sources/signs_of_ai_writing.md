# Signs of AI Writing

This page provides guidance on identifying text that may have been generated or significantly edited by artificial intelligence (AI) tools, particularly large language models like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

## Caveats

### AI detection tools

AI detection tools are not reliable. They often produce false positives and false negatives. Some tools may be designed to detect specific AI models but fail with others or newer versions. Academic studies have shown detection tools to be unreliable, with accuracy rates as low as 50-60%.

### Your detection ability

Even experienced editors may not reliably detect AI-generated text. Studies show that humans can identify AI writing at rates only slightly better than random chance. AI writing has evolved to be increasingly sophisticated, making detection more difficult.

## Content

### Undue emphasis on significance, legacy, and broader trends

AI writing often emphasizes the historical importance, lasting impact, or broader implications of topics, even when such emphasis is undue or inappropriate for the context.

### Undue emphasis on notability, attribution, and media coverage

AI tends to overemphasize how "notable" or "widely covered" a topic is, or attributes significance based on media attention rather than substantive importance.

### Superficial analyses

AI writing may provide surface-level analysis without deep insight, often repeating obvious points or failing to engage with complex nuances.

### Promotional and advertisement-like language

AI sometimes produces text that reads like marketing copy, with overly enthusiastic or sales-oriented language that doesn't fit encyclopedic writing.

### Vague attributions and overgeneralization of opinions

AI may attribute opinions or claims to vague groups ("many experts believe," "critics argue") without specific sourcing, or overgeneralize complex viewpoints.

### Outline-like conclusions about challenges and future prospects

AI writing often concludes articles with outline-style summaries of challenges and future prospects, presented in a structured but superficial manner.

### Leads treating Wikipedia lists or broad article titles as proper nouns

AI may treat generic terms or list titles as if they were proper nouns, capitalizing them inappropriately or treating them as specific entities.

### Vague see also sections

AI-generated see also sections may contain generic or irrelevant links that don't meaningfully relate to the article's content.

## Language and Grammar

### Overused "AI vocabulary" words

AI writing tends to overuse certain trendy or buzzwords like "revolutionize," "transformative," "pioneering," "innovative," "dynamic," "vibrant," "thriving," "landscape," "ecosystem," "narrative," "discourse," "paradigm," and "leverage."

### Avoidance of basic copulatives ("is"/"are" phrases)

AI often avoids simple "is/are" constructions, preferring more complex phrasing even when simple forms would be clearer.

### Negative parallelisms

AI frequently uses parallel structures with negative constructions, such as "not only... but also" or "neither... nor" patterns.

### Rule of three

AI often structures lists or examples in groups of three, following the "rule of three" rhetorical device.

### Elegant variation

AI tends to vary word choice excessively, using synonyms for the same concept even when repetition would be clearer or more appropriate.

### False ranges

AI may create false ranges or continua, such as "from X to Y" when the concepts aren't actually on a spectrum.

## Style

### Title case

AI often uses title case inappropriately, capitalizing words that shouldn't be capitalized in running text.

### Overuse of boldface

AI tends to overuse bold formatting for emphasis, even in contexts where it's not conventionally used.

### Inline-header vertical lists

AI may create vertical lists that function as headers within the text, rather than using proper section headers.

### Emoji

AI sometimes includes emoji in formal writing contexts where they would be inappropriate.

### Overuse of em dashes

AI frequently uses em dashes (—) excessively, often in places where commas, parentheses, or other punctuation would be more appropriate.

### Unusual use of tables

AI may create tables for information that would be better presented in prose or standard list formats.

### Curly quotation marks and apostrophes

AI often uses curly/smart quotation marks and apostrophes instead of straight ones, which may not match Wikipedia's style guidelines.

### Subject lines

AI may include email-style subject lines or headers that don't fit the context of Wikipedia articles.

## Communication Intended for the User

### Collaborative communication

AI writing may include phrases that seem designed to engage the reader collaboratively, such as "let's explore" or "consider this."

### Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers and speculation about gaps in sources

AI may include disclaimers about knowledge cutoffs or speculate about why certain information might be missing from sources.

### Phrasal templates and placeholder text

AI sometimes generates text that resembles template responses or placeholder content.

## Markup

### Use of Markdown

AI may use Markdown syntax instead of wikitext when editing Wikipedia.

### Broken wikitext

AI often produces malformed wikitext, with incorrect syntax for links, templates, or formatting.

#### Links to searches

AI may create links that go to search results rather than specific pages.

### Reference markup bugs: contentReference, oaicite, oai_citation, +1, attached_fil

AI may include malformed reference markup or citation codes that don't exist in Wikipedia's system.

#### Links to searches

AI may create links that go to search results rather than specific pages.

### attribution and attributableIndex

AI may include attribution markup that doesn't match Wikipedia's citation standards.

### Non-existent or out-of-place categories

AI may add categories that don't exist or are inappropriate for the article.

### Non-existent templates

AI may attempt to use templates that don't exist on Wikipedia.

## Citations

### Broken external links

AI often generates broken or malformed URLs in citations.

### Invalid DOI and ISBNs

AI may create fake or incorrect DOI and ISBN numbers.

### Outdated access-dates

AI citations may include access dates that are implausibly old or new.

### DOIs that lead to unrelated articles

AI may generate DOI links that don't match the cited work.

### Book citations without page numbers or URLs

AI may cite books without providing specific page numbers or source links.

### Incorrect or unconventional use of references

AI may misuse citation templates or place references inappropriately.

### utm_source=

AI citations may include tracking parameters like utm_source that are inappropriate for encyclopedic citations.

### Named references declared in references section but unused in article body

AI may create reference lists with entries that aren't actually cited in the text.

## Miscellaneous

### Sudden shift in writing style

Articles may show abrupt changes in writing quality or style, indicating AI editing of human-written text.

### Overwhelmingly verbose edit summaries

AI may generate excessively detailed edit summaries that don't match typical Wikipedia editing practices.

### "Submission statements" in AFC drafts

AI may include formal submission statements in Articles for Creation drafts.

### Pre-placed maintenance templates

AI may add maintenance templates before they're actually needed.

## Signs of Human Writing

### Age of text relative to ChatGPT launch

Text predating November 2022 (when ChatGPT launched) is unlikely to be AI-generated.

### Ability to explain one's own editorial choices

Human editors can typically explain their reasoning for specific edits or content decisions.

## Ineffective Indicators

Some commonly mentioned "signs" of AI writing are not reliable indicators:

- Perfect grammar (AI can have imperfect grammar, humans can have perfect grammar)
- Use of passive voice (both AI and humans use passive voice)
- Certain words or phrases (AI vocabulary evolves and humans also use these terms)
- Sentence length variety (both AI and humans vary sentence length)

## Historical Indicators

### Didactic disclaimers (2022–2024)

Early AI writing often included disclaimers about being AI-generated or not having real-time knowledge.

### Section summaries

Early AI writing sometimes summarized sections at their ends.

### Prompt refusal

Early AI models would refuse certain types of requests or topics.

### Abrupt cut offs

Early AI writing would sometimes end abruptly mid-sentence.
